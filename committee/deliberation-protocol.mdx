A deliberation round is not a summary of what the committee decided. It is a structured record of *how* the committee reached its conclusion — every position stated, every pitch made, every rebuttal issued, and every vote cast. The protocol exists to make the reasoning behind every architectural decision auditable, not just the outcome.

## What Full Documentation Means

Every round records all six members' views in full. The documentation rule is strict:

- Every pitch is recorded, attributed to the member who made it, in their own voice
- Every rebuttal is recorded with equal attribution and fidelity
- Where a member's position changed during the round, the change and its cause are recorded explicitly
- Concessions and withdrawn arguments are treated as the highest-value content a round produces — they mark where reasoning genuinely moved, which is where the architecture learned something

There is no summarization. A round that compresses "Isabella argued for stricter permission tiers" into a bullet has lost the architectural reasoning that made the argument worth recording. The full text is the record.

## Vote Arithmetic

The committee has six voting members. Votes are called after positions are fully stated and rebuttals are complete.

| Outcome | Result | Requirement |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| **6–0** unanimous | Carries | Full agreement noted in record |
| **5–1** majority | Carries | Dissenting position recorded in full |
| **4–2** majority | Carries | Both dissenting positions recorded in full |
| **3–3** split | Does **not** carry | Both sides compiled as steel-manned brief; escalated to Zachari |

A 3–3 split is not a failure state — it is a signal that the decision requires architectural authority that the committee is not structured to resolve alone. Both sides of the split receive equal steel-manning treatment before the brief goes to Zachari.

<Note>
  A unanimous 6–0 vote is not self-executing. Regardless of the vote result, Zachari's explicit approval is always required before a selection becomes the focus of the next build. A carried vote makes a selection *eligible*; it does not scope it.
</Note>

## From Vote to Build Focus

The distinction between *eligible* and *scoped* is load-bearing in the governance model.

1. **Deliberation round concludes** — all positions recorded, vote called
2. **Vote carries** (or Zachari resolves a split) — selection becomes eligible
3. **Zachari reviews** the full deliberation record, not just the vote result
4. **Zachari approves** — the selection is assigned as the focus of the next build cycle
5. **Build begins** — subagents are dispatched with the adoption preamble pasted verbatim from `Subagents/subagent-adoption-preamble.md`

No step in this chain is optional. A 6–0 vote with no Zachari approval produces no build.

## Round Structure

Each round in the governance journal follows a consistent structure. Use this as a reference when reading or contributing to the record.

```
Round [N] — [Topic or Decision Under Deliberation]

Context
  Brief framing of what triggered the round and what is being decided.

Member Positions
  [Member Name] — [Initial Position]
    Full statement of their position, in their own voice.
  [Member Name] — [Initial Position]
    ...

Pitches
  [Member Name]
    Full pitch for their preferred resolution, attributed.

Rebuttals
  [Member Name] → [Member Name]
    Full rebuttal, attributed to both the speaker and the target.

Position Changes
  [Member Name] — changed from [X] to [Y]
    Cause of the change recorded explicitly.

Concessions and Withdrawn Arguments
  [Member Name] — withdrew [argument]
    What changed their reasoning.

Vote
  [Member]: [Position]
  [Member]: [Position]
  ...
  Result: [6–0 / 5–1 / 4–2 / 3–3 escalated]

Dissent Record (if applicable)
  Full dissenting position(s), attributed.

Zachari's Decision (after review)
  [Approved / Modified / Deferred] — with rationale.
```

## The Append-Only Journal

The full deliberation record lives at `Subagents/committee-deliberation.md`. The journal spans 38+ rounds and covers every major architectural decision in the framework's history, including the v1.6 expansion that added Søren Aalto and Renata Ferreira.

The journal is **append-only**. No round is ever edited, redacted, or removed. The value of the record comes from its completeness — a decision made in Round 12 that was revisited in Round 31 is only intelligible if Round 12's full reasoning is still present.

## The Document-Truth Audit

Every build cycle is gated by the Document-Truth Audit, a four-part consistency check that Renata Ferreira oversees as part of the integration verification mandate. The audit verifies that all operative documents agree on:

1. **Counts** — member counts, rule counts, tier counts, and any other enumerated quantities match across all documents
2. **Rules** — every rule stated in one document appears with equivalent meaning in every document that references it
3. **Capability labels** — labels used to describe model capabilities are consistent and do not drift between documents
4. **Declared target models** — the models a build targets are declared identically across all operative documents

A build that fails any of the four checks does not proceed. The audit is not a post-build review — it is a pre-build gate. Discrepancies surface before code ships, not after.
